Claude agents made LFM3-1.7B better by editing 0.002% of its weights by hand

No gradients, no fine-tuning, no fitting to a loss. AI editors inspected the model's activations, wrote small gated units directly into its MLP weights, and competed in a genetic loop. The surviving edit raises held-out MMLU-Pro accuracy from 33.6% to 45.3% and transfers to GPQA Diamond, a benchmark never used for tuning.

What was replicated

This reproduces Experiment 8 of Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLM editors change a model's weights directly, using Python over the weights and a small budget of forward passes, and the best edit becomes the parent of the next generation.

  • Model: LFM3-1.7B (24 layers, hybrid convolution and attention). The embedding, output head and final norm were off limits.
  • Editors: Claude, working as one editor plus independent Claude sub-agent editors that could not read each other's work.
  • Budget per editor and generation: 6 forward passes (teacher-forced reads with activations, or generation runs), at most 1 submitted edit.
  • Rules (same as the paper): no training, no optimizers, no fitting weights to a loss even in closed form, no running or simulating the network outside the forward tool.
  • Selection: a candidate replaces the champion only if its paired gain on 128 hidden MMLU-Pro questions has z ≥ 2. Otherwise the champion survives.
  • Hardware: one 4-core CPU with 15 GB RAM, no GPU. Generation runs on a deterministic llama.cpp build (Q8_0) so every comparison is paired with identical seeds.

What the model was getting wrong

In thinking mode, the unedited model rarely fails for lack of knowledge. It fails because it does not stop. On held-out questions, 56% of its answers ran out of the token budget; when it did finish, it was right 77% of the time.

  • Reopening a settled answer. It writes "Thus the answer is (I)." and then "But let's double-check…". In finished traces the first firm conclusion is the final answer in 38 of 39 cases.
  • Oscillating between two finalists. After "so it's B or D", it reopens the comparison instead of committing.
  • Re-deriving everything in the visible answer. After closing its thinking, it often repeats the whole solution and gets cut off inside the answer.

The edit that survived

Three gated units, each a single repurposed MLP neuron (plus folding near-duplicate neurons to free slots). Each gate reads a direction in the residual stream that marks one specific situation and stays silent elsewhere; each output writes toward the tokens that end that failure.

What we learned about editing weights

  • The mechanisms are sharp. The situations the units detect separate from all other tokens with AUC 0.96–0.99 in a single residual-stream direction, and in read passes the edited units push the targeted token from near-zero to near-certain probability at those points.
  • No edit is silent. Repurposing any neuron removes its old output, and under sampling that tiny change sends most traces down a different path early on, typically within the first fifth of the text. Each edit therefore re-rolls many answers, which adds a noise floor of about ±2.5 pp at 128 questions.
  • Small mechanisms add up. Two edits that each failed alone passed when stacked, because the re-roll cost is paid once.
  • Measure on fresh questions. Gains on questions the editors had studied overstated the true effect by 2–3 standard errors. A second untouched question bank predicted selection results to within one standard error.

Caveat on the token budget. All results above use a 3,072-token cap on thinking plus answer, chosen so that runs fit on a CPU. The paper used 32,768 tokens. Much of the gain comes from finishing within the budget: the cut share fell from 56% to 46% on MMLU-Pro and from 85% to 76% on GPQA. With a longer budget, some answers the base model loses here would finish, so the gap is likely smaller.

In progress: both models are being re-run on the same 128 held-out questions with an 8,192-token cap. This page will be updated with the result.

Methods in brief

  • Thinking mode with the LFM3 model card's sampling (temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20) and a fixed seed per question shared by all models.
  • Graded on the last "answer is (X)" after the thinking. An answer cut at the cap counts as wrong.
  • Selection questions are drawn fresh each generation and never shown to editors. Held-out MMLU-Pro (128) and GPQA Diamond (198) were never used for selection or design.
  • Every number is a single deterministic run per model. Standard errors are paired across questions.
